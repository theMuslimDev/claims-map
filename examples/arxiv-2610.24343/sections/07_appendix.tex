\subsection{Model and Training Parameters}
\label{model_and_training_params}
Model and training parameters are summarized in Tables \ref{model_params} and \ref{train_params}. In our experiments, both models were trained using AdamW with the Warmup-Stable-Decay learning-rate schedule, with annealing over the final 20\% of the token horizon for Nemotron 3 Nano and the final 15\% for Nemotron-H 8B. Each model was trained to a maximum of 1T tokens; some ablations were stopped early in the schedule. Weight decay of 0.1 was used for all models. Attention layers used Grouped Query Attention. All models have a sequence length of 8192.

\subsection{Downstream Task Accuracy and Run-to-Run Variability}

To determine whether differences in training and validation loss gaps and downstream task accuracy exceed expected
run-to-run variability, we trained the \nvrecipe recipe three times and evaluated each
resulting model on the same downstream tasks. 

\subsubsection{Loss Gap Variability}
\label{app:loss-variability}
We measure run-to-run loss variability as
$\max_{a \neq b} |L_a-L_b|/L_b$ across the three repeated runs, where $L$ is the loss measured at the 1T-token horizon.
For the Nemotron 3 Nano model, this variability is approximately \(0.06\%\) in training loss and \(0.04\%\) in validation loss; for the Nemotron-H 8B model, it is \(0.027\%\) and \(0.064\%\), respectively. Loss differences substantially exceeding these ranges are unlikely to be explained by run-to-run variability alone.

\subsubsection{Downstream Task Accuracy}
For model $m$ and task $t$, we
estimate the relative noise tolerance as
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon_{m,t} =
    \frac{\max_i s_{m,t,i} - \min_i s_{m,t,i}}
         {\max_i s_{m,t,i}},
\end{equation}
where $s_{m,t,i}$ is the score from repeated run $i$. We then calculate the
relative difference between \newrecipe and the \nvrecipe baseline:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{m,t} =
    \frac{s_{m,t}^{\mathrm{Mosaic}} -
          s_{m,t}^{\mathrm{NV25}}}
         {s_{m,t}^{\mathrm{NV25}}}.
\end{equation}
A positive $\Delta_{m,t}$ indicates that \newrecipe improves accuracy.
Differences with $\lvert\Delta_{m,t}\rvert > \epsilon_{m,t}$
are considered outside the corresponding noise reference. Because this estimate is based on only three runs, the measured range may understate the true run-to-run variability.

For each aggregate category $C$, we report the mean of
its constituent task scores and compute the relative difference
between the resulting NVIDIA~'25 NVFP4 and Mosaic NVFP4 category scores.
This aggregate relative difference can equivalently be written as a
weighted average of the task-level relative differences:
\[
\Delta_{m,C}
=
\sum_{t \in C} w_{m,t}\Delta_{m,t}
=
\frac{
\bar{s}^{\,\mathrm{Mosaic}}_{m,C}
-
\bar{s}^{\,\mathrm{NV25}}_{m,C}
}{
\bar{s}^{\,\mathrm{NV25}}_{m,C}
},
\qquad
w_{m,t}
=
\frac{s^{\mathrm{NV25}}_{m,t}}
{\sum_{j \in C}s^{\mathrm{NV25}}_{m,j}}.
\]
Here, $w_{m,t}$ is the normalized contribution of task $t$ to the
aggregate NVIDIA~'25 NVFP4 score. We use the same weights to combine
the task-level noise tolerances:
\[
\widetilde{\epsilon}_{m,C}
=
\sum_{t \in C}w_{m,t}\epsilon_{m,t}.
\]

We use $\widetilde{\epsilon}_{m,C}$ as a score-aligned
aggregate noise reference.
